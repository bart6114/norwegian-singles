# Choose your week & run the sessions {#implementing-the-method}

## Choose your week {#choose-your-week}

Start with the running time and number of days you have recently sustained. The standard is three quality sessions, separated by easy days, plus an easy longer run. Use the transition if you already run regularly but are new to structured intensity. If you are still establishing regular running, build that routine first.

The schedules below are worked examples for this guide, not copied schedules from a book or universal starting doses. Their interval structure follows the practice discussed by sirpoc; the time budgets show how to fit it into a week. His [posts #562, #563, and #571](https://www.letsrun.com/forum/flat_read.php?thread=12130781&page=28) explain approximate pacing, time-based repetitions, and scaling work to weekly volume.

### Standard week: five hours {#standard-week}

For a runner already comfortable with about five hours over seven days and able to recover between quality sessions. A, B, and C refer to the complete session cards below. All times are minutes.

| Day | Session | Running | Quality |
|:----|:--------|--------:|--------:|
| Monday | Easy | 30 | 0 |
| Tuesday | A: short repetitions | 50 | 24 |
| Wednesday | Easy | 30 | 0 |
| Thursday | B: medium repetitions | 50 | 24 |
| Friday | Easy | 30 | 0 |
| Saturday | C: long repetitions | 50 | 24 |
| Sunday | Longer easy | 60 | 0 |
| Total | 5 hours; 24% quality | 300 | 72 |

### Transition: two quality sessions {#transition-week}

For an established runner with similar running time who is learning controlled intervals. Saturday stays easy. The smaller quality share is intentional; there is no need to fill a quota while adapting.

| Day | Session | Running | Quality |
|:----|:--------|--------:|--------:|
| Monday | Easy | 30 | 0 |
| Tuesday | A: short repetitions | 50 | 24 |
| Wednesday | Easy | 30 | 0 |
| Thursday | B: medium repetitions | 50 | 24 |
| Friday | Easy | 30 | 0 |
| Saturday | Easy | 50 | 0 |
| Sunday | Longer easy | 60 | 0 |
| Total | 5 hours; 16% quality | 300 | 48 |

If these sessions exceed your current experience, use the shorter cards and keep your existing easy routine. Moving to the standard example replaces Saturday's 50-minute easy run with a 50-minute quality session. Total running time stays the same, while quality work rises; keep the other sessions and paces steady. Make that change only once the two-session routine is repeatable and recovery is reliable.

### Larger week: seven hours {#larger-week}

For someone who already sustains about seven hours and tolerates the standard structure. Use the larger version of each card. This is a separate example, not the next week's target after the five-hour schedule.

| Day | Session | Running | Quality |
|:----|:--------|--------:|--------:|
| Monday | Easy | 50 | 0 |
| Tuesday | A: larger | 60 | 30 |
| Wednesday | Easy | 50 | 0 |
| Thursday | B: larger | 60 | 30 |
| Friday | Easy | 50 | 0 |
| Saturday | C: larger | 60 | 30 |
| Sunday | Longer easy | 90 | 0 |
| Total | 7 hours; 21.4% quality | 420 | 90 |

Build additional easy time before enlarging the quality sessions. The [progression section](#progression) explains what to hold steady at each step.

### A week with a rest day {#rest-day-week}

If six days fit better, keep Friday free and use the shorter cards. This four-hour example is for a runner already accustomed to three quality days who needs a smaller week. Someone new to intervals should keep the two-session transition and make Saturday easy instead.

| Day | Session | Running | Quality |
|:----|:--------|--------:|--------:|
| Monday | Easy | 30 | 0 |
| Tuesday | A: shorter | 40 | 18 |
| Wednesday | Easy | 30 | 0 |
| Thursday | B: shorter | 40 | 18 |
| Friday | Rest | 0 | 0 |
| Saturday | C: shorter | 40 | 18 |
| Sunday | Longer easy | 60 | 0 |
| Total | 4 hours; 22.5% quality | 240 | 54 |

If fewer days are available, reduce the number of quality sessions instead of putting them back to back. Do not squeeze a full seven-day schedule into four running days.

## Count the time once {#count-the-time}

Quality minutes are the work repetitions only. Total running time includes those repetitions, running warm-ups and cool-downs, easy runs, and jogging recoveries. Quality share is quality minutes divided by total running minutes. This convention makes the examples comparable; it is not the same as a watch's time-in-heart-rate-zones report.

Around 20-25% is a planning range used in the described practice, not a minimum to meet every week or a guarantee of a suitable dose. The transition deliberately sits below it. Races and harder sessions also need to be accounted for when planning recovery, even though they are not sub-threshold minutes. [Sirpoc on balancing easy and quality running, post #478](https://www.letsrun.com/forum/flat_read.php?thread=12130781&page=23).

These examples use jogging recoveries. A set of eight repetitions has seven recoveries, followed immediately by the cool-down. If you stand or walk instead, include those breaks in elapsed session time but exclude them from running time. For example, card A would take 50 minutes elapsed with 43 minutes running and 24 minutes quality if all seven recoveries were standing. Recalculate the whole week when changing the recovery convention.

## Run the sessions {#session-cards}

Choose the card assigned in your week. Each has a shorter and a larger version; run one version, not all three. These are editorial time budgets built around short recoveries and different repetition lengths. Sirpoc describes [60-second recoveries in his earlier sessions, post #231](https://www.letsrun.com/forum/flat_read.php?thread=12130781&page=11); the exact totals below are this guide's examples.

Use the [LacTrace pace guide](https://lactrace.com/norwegian-singles) with a recent race result and the appropriate repetition duration. Select a controlled starting pace, then follow [the effort checks](section1_core_principles.md#intensity-control). If you lack a representative result, begin conservatively by effort; wait until you have a stable running routine before scheduling a race or time trial to calibrate pace.

### A: short repetitions {#session-a}

Use this on Tuesday. It offers frequent checks on effort and rhythm. The first repetitions should feel restrained; low early heart rate is no reason to speed up.

1. Run easily for 10 minutes.
2. Run 8 repetitions of 3 minutes at the guide's short-repetition starting pace. Jog easily for 1 minute between repetitions.
3. Run easily for 9 minutes to cool down.

Time check: 10 + (8 x 3) + (7 x 1) + 9 = 50 minutes running; 24 minutes quality.

For a shorter session, warm up for 10 minutes, run 6 x 3 minutes with five 1-minute jogs, and cool down for 7 minutes.

Time check: 10 + (6 x 3) + (5 x 1) + 7 = 40 minutes running; 18 minutes quality.

For the larger week, warm up for 12 minutes, run 10 x 3 minutes with nine 1-minute jogs, and cool down for 9 minutes.

Time check: 12 + (10 x 3) + (9 x 1) + 9 = 60 minutes running; 30 minutes quality.

If you are straining to finish the early repetitions or need longer breaks to hold the pace, slow down. Stop the work early if control does not return; keep the cool-down easy if you are well enough to continue.

### B: medium repetitions {#session-b}

Use this on Thursday. Longer uninterrupted work gives you time to judge whether breathing and effort settle. Choose the pace for six-minute repetitions; do not assume Tuesday's pace will fit.

1. Run easily for 12 minutes.
2. Run 4 repetitions of 6 minutes with 1 minute of easy jogging between them.
3. Run easily for 11 minutes to cool down.

Time check: 12 + (4 x 6) + (3 x 1) + 11 = 50 minutes running; 24 minutes quality.

For a shorter session, warm up for 10 minutes, run 3 x 6 minutes with two 1-minute jogs, and cool down for 10 minutes.

Time check: 10 + (3 x 6) + (2 x 1) + 10 = 40 minutes running; 18 minutes quality.

For the larger week, warm up for 12 minutes, run 5 x 6 minutes with four 1-minute jogs, and cool down for 14 minutes.

Time check: 12 + (5 x 6) + (4 x 1) + 14 = 60 minutes running; 30 minutes quality.

If each repetition becomes noticeably harder at the same pace, slow down before the next one. You should finish with reserve, not need to lie down after the last repetition.

### C: long repetitions {#session-c}

Use this on Saturday in the three-session week. Keep the effort controlled throughout the longer blocks and leave enough recovery for Sunday's easy run. Use the pace range for the actual work duration: nine, ten, or twelve minutes depending on the version.

1. Run easily for 12 minutes.
2. Run 2 repetitions of 12 minutes, with 1 minute of easy jogging between them.
3. Run easily for 13 minutes to cool down.

Time check: 12 + (2 x 12) + (1 x 1) + 13 = 50 minutes running; 24 minutes quality.

For a shorter session, warm up for 10 minutes, run 2 x 9 minutes with a 1-minute jog between them, and cool down for 11 minutes.

Time check: 10 + (2 x 9) + (1 x 1) + 11 = 40 minutes running; 18 minutes quality.

For the larger week, warm up for 12 minutes, run 3 x 10 minutes with two 2-minute jogs, and cool down for 14 minutes.

Time check: 12 + (3 x 10) + (2 x 2) + 14 = 60 minutes running; 30 minutes quality.

If the first block already feels like a race effort, slow down or end the quality work. The recovery should let you restart smoothly; it does not make an excessive pace appropriate.

## Progression {#progression}

Repeat a manageable week until you understand how you recover. Consistent execution and normal easy days are better reasons to progress than a date on the calendar. If recovery is uneven, keep the dose unchanged or reduce it.

| Decision | What changes | What stays steady |
|:---------|:-------------|:------------------|
| You can accommodate more running and recover well | Add a small amount of easy running within the time you can sustain | Quality minutes and paces |
| You have built a repeatable week with room for more quality | Replace some existing easy time with a little more work, or move from the transition to three sessions | Total time initially, and pace |
| A recent race or time trial shows changed fitness | Refresh the pace estimate and check it over subsequent sessions | Repetition count and weekly time |
| Sessions feel harder or recovery is inconsistent | Hold the week steady, shorten quality, or replace a quality day with easy running/rest | Do not force faster paces |

The larger week illustrates a different established volume. It is not an instruction to jump from five to seven hours. There is no universal weekly percentage increase here. When extending one session, account for its warm-up and recoveries as well as the added work.

A cool day after weeks of heat may produce faster running at the same effort without proving a new fitness level. Compare conditions and repeated sessions before changing pace. When you use a race to recalibrate, [replace a quality session and allow recovery](section5_applicability_distances.md#race-week).

If progress stalls, work through [the recovery and execution checks](section6_benefits_monitoring.md#stagnation) before adding more training.
