# Start here {#start-here .unnumbered}

Norwegian Singles centres on three controlled sub-threshold sessions spread through the week, with easy running between them and an easy longer run. The aim is to build a routine you can repeat. Start with the running you already do, then choose a week that fits your experience and available time.

| Days | Run |
|:-----|:----|
| Monday, Wednesday, Friday | Easy |
| Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday | Quality |
| Sunday | Longer easy |

Quality means intervals at a controlled effort below your second lactate threshold. You should finish with enough in reserve to repeat the routine later in the week. Easy runs add running time while leaving you ready for the next session. The longer run stays easy too.

1. [Choose your starting week](section2_implementing_the_method.md#choose-your-week). Compare the standard week, a transition with two quality sessions, and a larger week.
2. [Run your first session](section2_implementing_the_method.md#session-cards). Follow a complete workout, including warm-up, recoveries, and cool-down.
3. [Adjust your training](section6_benefits_monitoring.md#adjust-your-week). Decide what to do when you miss a run, feel unusually tired, or have less time.

## Before you start

If you already run regularly, use your recent, repeatable week as the starting point. An experienced runner changing methods may be ready for three quality sessions; someone new to structured intensity can use the two-session transition. If running itself is new or irregular, establish an easy routine before adding these workouts.

You need a timer, a way to judge pace or effort, and somewhere to record your runs. A recent race result can help set starting paces. Heart-rate data can help with checks during a session; a lactate meter or paid training platform is optional. Read [how to control effort](section1_core_principles.md#intensity-control) before your first workout.

## Background & conceptualization

This guide grew out of the LetsRun discussion about adapting Norwegian threshold training to recreational running. James Copeland, known as sirpoc84, shared his training and helped popularize the singles approach in that community. His account drew on his own experiments and observations of Kristoffer Ingebrigtsen's running.

The version described here uses three single quality sessions per week. Marius Bakken's work on lactate-controlled training is part of its background; his double-threshold model and the different Ingebrigtsen routines have their own schedules and context.

- James Copeland's book, [Norwegian Singles Method: Subthreshold Running Kept Simple](https://mybook.to/XzwWbK3), has a foreword by Marius Bakken.
- The [original LetsRun thread](https://www.letsrun.com/forum/flat_read.php?thread=12130781) contains the discussion from which this guide began.
- The [Strava group](https://strava.app.link/QyAqunp07Pb) and [r/NorwegianSinglesRun](https://www.reddit.com/r/NorwegianSinglesRun/) host ongoing community discussion.
- The [LacTrace pace guide](https://lactrace.com/norwegian-singles) provides starting estimates from current fitness.

See [Sources & background](sources.md#sources-and-background) for more references and how this guide uses them.
